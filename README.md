## `01_manifesto`

A viral sequence enters as text.  
A model compresses it into coordinates.  
Somewhere in that compression, molecular similarity, evolutionary memory, temporal drift, and taxonomic structure either survive or disappear.

My work is about auditing that space.

I am a mathematician and computer scientist working at the intersection of **AI for Biology**, **scientific machine learning**, **bioinformatics**, and **stochastic modeling**.

My current research studies how biological foundation models organize viral sequence information in latent space, with emphasis on **Influenza A HA/NA**, **AntigenLM representations**, **latent geometry**, and **continuous-time stochastic models of antigenic drift**.

I care about systems where geometry, probability, and computation do not merely decorate the interface — they form the analytical core beneath it.

```yaml
operating_principles:
  - rigor before hype
  - biological signal before aesthetic plots
  - interpretable geometry before black-box confidence
  - stochastic models before deterministic storytelling
  - research into systems
```

## `04_latent_geometry`

**Geometric audit of AntigenLM representations for Influenza A HA/NA.**

This project studies whether AntigenLM latent spaces preserve biologically meaningful structure across molecular similarity, temporal organization, subtype behavior, intrinsic dimension, and evolutionary-taxonomic neighborhoods.

It treats the embedding space as an object of scientific inspection: not a visualization endpoint, but a biological hypothesis surface.

```yaml
object:
  name: latent_geometry
  model: AntigenLM
  biological_domain: Influenza A HA/NA
  questions:
    - Do learned neighborhoods preserve molecular similarity?
    - Does temporal structure survive embedding compression?
    - Are subtype and taxonomic patterns geometrically detectable?
    - Can latent space support downstream stochastic modeling?
```

## `05_antigensde`

**Stochastic modeling of antigenic drift over learned biological representations.**

AntigenSDE explores continuous-time latent dynamics for Influenza A evolution, combining biological sequence embeddings with stochastic differential equations, competitive fitness landscapes, Monte Carlo simulation, and uncertainty-aware trajectories.

The goal is not to decorate embeddings with dynamics, but to ask whether latent biological spaces can sustain a useful mathematical model of motion.

```txt
latent state · drift field · diffusion term · fitness landscape · trajectory ensemble
```
